Did you really win?

Paste the message: an email, WhatsApp or SMS saying you won the EuroMillions, the Christmas lottery, an ONCE draw, a phone company promo or a car, even though you never bought a ticket. Then it asks for a tax, a courier fee or your bank details to “release” the prize. In one second you get a red-flag score and what to do before you reply.

Check a “you have won” message

Runs 100% in your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere. It is a rule-based awareness tool: a low score does not prove the prize is real. Real lotteries never ask you to pay anything to collect a prize; check your ticket yourself on the operator’s official website or at an official lottery outlet.

    What to do before you reply
      No ticket, no prize

      You cannot win a lottery you never entered. “Your email or phone number was selected in a random draw” is the opening line of the scam, not of a win.

      Real prizes never cost money

      In Spain, tax on big lottery prizes is withheld from the prize itself. Nobody asks you to pay a tax, notary, customs or courier fee in advance to receive it.

      Official look, fake source

      Logos, stamps, case numbers and a PDF “winning certificate” take minutes to fake. Check the operator’s real website yourself instead of the link or number in the message.
