How to spot scams on TikTok: 8 patterns to know

By Ali Attia (@alibadmoney) · Updated: 2026-09-25

Most TikTok scams do not happen on TikTok. The video or comment is the hook; the money moves somewhere else: a DM, a WhatsApp group, a Telegram channel, a payment outside the app. If you learn the eight patterns below, you will recognise nearly every version of them.

8 common TikTok scam patterns

  1. Impersonator giveaways. A copy of a creator's account replies to comments: "Congrats, you won, message this account to claim." The real creator did not write it. See how to spot a fake giveaway.
  2. "Get paid to like videos" task jobs. You are paid small amounts for simple tasks at first. Then you are told to deposit money to unlock "premium tasks" or to withdraw your earnings. The early payments are the bait.
  3. Deepfake or re-uploaded crypto livestreams. A famous founder "live" on a new account, with a QR code or address on screen and a promise to double what you send.
  4. Trading mentors and "copy my trades". Screenshots of profits, a rented car, and a link to a VIP group. The product is the subscription, the course or the broker sign-up that pays the mentor a commission.
  5. Off-app deals. Designer items, phones or concert tickets far below the normal price, but only if you pay by transfer, crypto or a friends-and-family payment outside the platform, where you have no buyer protection.
  6. "Wrong number" and friendly strangers. A new contact who moves the chat to WhatsApp, builds a friendship over days, then introduces an investment app that shows fake growing balances until you try to withdraw.
  7. Fake brand deals and creator programmes. "We want to sponsor you, just pay for the sample kit" or "pay a fee to get verified". Real brands pay creators; they do not charge them.
  8. "We recover your money" comments. Under videos about scams, accounts offer to recover lost funds for an upfront fee. This targets people who were already scammed.

5 quick checks before you trust a TikTok offer

  1. Who gets paid if you say yes? If the creator earns from your sign-up, deposit or subscription, the video is an ad, whatever it looks like.
  2. Does it move you off the app? "DM me", "join my Telegram", "message me on WhatsApp". Moving off-platform removes moderation and buyer protection.
  3. Do you have to pay first? Fees to unlock, withdraw, verify or claim are the scam.
  4. Can you verify it outside TikTok? Search the company, the regulator's register or the official brand site yourself.
  5. Does the math work? "1% a day" sounds small. Compounded for a year it is about 38 times your money. Paste the claim into the scam checker and it does the math for you.

How to report on TikTok

Press and hold the video or comment, choose Report, then pick the closest reason (scams or fraud, impersonation). To report an account, open the profile, tap the share arrow or the three dots and choose Report. If you lost money, also contact your bank and the police. In Spain, INCIBE's free helpline 017 gives advice.

FAQ

Are TikTok "paid to like" jobs real?

Legitimate companies do not pay random people to like videos and then ask them to deposit money. If a job asks you to pay to unlock tasks or withdraw earnings, it is a task scam.

Is a trading mentor with profit screenshots a scam?

Not always, but screenshots prove nothing. Ask for a third-party verified live track record, check the broker on the regulator's register, and find out whether the mentor earns from your deposit.

How can I tell if a livestream is a deepfake?

Check the account: a brand-new or renamed account streaming a famous person is a red flag. Any live that asks you to send crypto to an address to get more back is a scam, whether or not the video is real.

Does TikTok refund money lost to scams?

Payments made outside the app are generally not covered. Contact your bank or card provider immediately; they are your best chance of stopping or disputing a payment.
