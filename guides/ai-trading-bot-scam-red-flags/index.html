AI trading bot scams: 7 red flags before you pay

By Ali Attia (@alibadmoney) · Updated: 2026-09-25

"Fully automated AI bot turns $50 into $400 a week." Trading bots are real software, but the way many of them are sold follows the same script: a borrowed brand name, a win rate instead of a profit figure, screenshots instead of verified results, and a partner broker that pays the seller for your deposit. Here are the red flags, with the math.

The 7 red flags

  1. A win rate instead of a profit figure. Win 1 dollar 95 times and lose 30 dollars 5 times: that is a 95% win rate and a loss of 55 dollars. Ask for net profit, risk per trade and maximum drawdown.
  2. Backtests and screenshots instead of live, verified results. A backtest is the strategy tested on past data, where it is easy to look perfect. Ask for a live account tracked by an independent third party over many months.
  3. Guaranteed or fixed returns. "1% a day, guaranteed." Compounded for a year, 1% a day is about 38 times your money (1.01365 ≈ 37.8). Nobody with that would sell it to you for 99 dollars.
  4. A borrowed brand name. "Grok", "ChatGPT" or a famous founder's name on a bot borrows trust. Those companies do not sell trading bots through Telegram. Check the official site of the brand.
  5. You must deposit with their "partner broker". The seller often earns a commission on your deposit or your trading volume, whether you win or lose. Check the broker yourself on the regulator's register (UK: FCA register; US: NFA BASIC; Spain: CNMV).
  6. Withdrawal problems. Balances that grow fast in an app, then a "tax", "fee" or "verification deposit" to withdraw. Paying to withdraw is the scam.
  7. VIP groups and pressure. Limited spots, countdowns, DMs from "account managers". Real tools do not need urgency.

Questions to ask before paying for any bot

  • What is the net profit, the maximum drawdown and the risk per trade over at least 12 months of live trading?
  • Who verifies the results, and can I see the tracking link myself?
  • Which regulated broker holds the money, and in whose name is the account?
  • Does the seller earn from my deposit or trading volume?
  • Can I withdraw at any time without paying anything extra?

If you already paid or deposited

Stop depositing, including any "fee to withdraw". Contact your bank or card provider, save screenshots of every conversation and payment, and report it to the police and to the financial regulator in your country. Be careful with anyone who contacts you offering to recover the money for a fee.

FAQ

Are all AI trading bots scams?

No. Automated trading software exists and some traders use it. The red flags are in how it is sold: guaranteed returns, win rates without losses, unverified results and a broker that pays the seller.

What is a realistic return for a trading bot?

There is no honest fixed number. Any seller who promises a specific daily or weekly return is making a promise no regulated firm is allowed to make.

How do I check if a broker is regulated?

Search the broker's legal name on the official register of your country's financial regulator, such as the FCA register in the UK or the CNMV in Spain, and check the regulator's warning lists.

Is "Grok Gold" an official xAI product?

Not that we know of. Bots using the names of AI companies are generally not affiliated with them. Check the company's official website before trusting any claim.
